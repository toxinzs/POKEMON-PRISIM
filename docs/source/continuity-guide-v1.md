**LOCKED** — Explicitly decided and approved. Treat it as canon.

**WORKING IDEA** — Being considered, but it can still change. Do not treat it as canon, and do not build major story events on it without confirming first.

**NEEDS DEVELOPMENT** — Something that has to be figured out, either before the RP starts or before the story reaches it.

**NOT IMPORTANT YET** — Something that can be invented naturally if and when it becomes relevant.

# PART 1 — THE WORLD

## 1.1 WHAT THIS PROJECT IS

PRISM is set in **the real modern world, where Pokémon have always existed alongside humanity.**

It is NOT a story where someone gets transported into the Pokémon world.

It is NOT the anime or game world with real place names pasted on top.

It is: **what the actual modern world would look like if Pokémon had always been part of it.**

Real countries, cities, governments, cultures, languages, currencies, businesses, schools, hospitals, transit, airports, laws, social media, news outlets, and technology all exist. Pokémon are real living creatures, and society has adapted around them for generations.

The fictional elements are the Pokémon-related systems and the fictional characters, organizations, and events built around them. Everything else should be as real as possible.

## 1.2 CORE REALISM PHILOSOPHY

The world is realistic **within the fact that Pokémon are real.** The constant question is:

> "If Pokémon had actually existed for generations, how would humans have adapted?"

Realism does NOT mean over-explaining. Most worldbuilding lives in the background. If Pokémon registration exists, a character just says **"Did you register him yet?"** and that's enough. If phones run on Rotom-derived technology, nobody explains it every time someone picks one up.

This guide exists to prevent contradictions. The story itself should still feel natural.

## 1.3 THE WORLD IS EARTH

When the story takes place somewhere real, use the real location: real airports, train lines, bus routes, neighborhoods, landmarks, stores, restaurants, and hotels. Use realistic contemporary prices in the local currency. If something is time-sensitive or current, research it rather than inventing it.

## 1.4 POKÉMON REGIONS

Traditional Pokémon regions are **geographical and cultural regions of Earth**, not separate continents or dimensions.

* **Kanto** — Japan's Kanto region (Tokyo, Yokohama, and so on)
* **Johto** — the corresponding area of Japan (Kansai)
* **Hoenn** — broadly Kyushu and surrounding geography
* **Sinnoh** — broadly Hokkaido
* **Unova** — New York and the northeastern United States
* **Kalos** — France
* **Alola** — Hawaii
* **Galar** — primarily the United Kingdom
* **Paldea** — primarily Spain and Iberia

Region names and real names are used interchangeably and naturally. "I'm going to Japan" and "I'm going to Kanto" can both be true.

**Administrative rule:** the **Unova League administratively covers the entire United States.** As a *place*, "Unova" still means NYC and the Northeast. Virginia is not in any canon region, but Nova falls under the Unova League. The Unova League is headquartered in New York.

Exact regional boundaries are NOT IMPORTANT YET.

## 1.5 REGIONS AND CULTURE

Each region keeps its Pokémon identity while being fully its real-world counterpart. Galar is British. Kalos is French. Alola is Hawaiian and Pacific Islander. Unova is American. Paldea is Iberian. Kanto is Japanese.

Traveling between regions involves real differences in language, accent, slang, food, architecture, climate, transit, laws, currency, customs, Pokémon populations, battle culture, and history. **Pokémon are a layer of culture, not a replacement for it.**

## 1.6 POKÉMON IN SOCIETY

Pokémon are not newly discovered. Most people grow up knowing them, and basic Pokémon safety is taught from childhood.

**Not everyone owns a Pokémon.** Ownership is a responsibility.

Pokémon can be companions, family Pokémon, working partners, service Pokémon, farm help, transportation, competitive battlers, research partners, rescue partners, security partners, therapy Pokémon, and professional partners.

**A Pokémon does not have to battle.** The world does not revolve around battling.

## 1.7 REAL ANIMALS — LOCKED

1. **Coexistence, not replacement.** Pokémon and real animals share ecosystems. Pokémon fill their own niches alongside animals and don't erase them. A marina has seagulls *and* Wingull. A park has squirrels *and* Skwovet.
2. **Everyday visibility.** Real animals (pigeons, deer, stray cats) are still the common background wildlife. Pokémon are everywhere too, but they're what people *notice*. A wild Pokémon in a city is normal. A rare one is an event.
3. **Pets.** Dogs and cats are still the most common household pets, because they're cheaper, need no registration, and fit apartments. A Pokémon is a bigger commitment: registered, regulated, and sometimes dangerous.
4. **Food and farming.** Real livestock (cows, chickens, pigs) are the main meat source. Eating Pokémon is culturally taboo in most places, with rare regional exceptions possible (canon Slowpoke Tail). Pokémon are used for products and labor: Miltank milk, Wooloo and Mareep wool, Combee honey, and plowing, herding, and hauling.
5. **Wild food chain.** Wild Pokémon eat berries, plants, and sometimes real animals, like a Pelipper diving for fish or a Mightyena hunting deer. It is shown naturally and never explained.
6. **Law split.** Real animals fall under real-world animal law (dog licensing, hunting seasons, animal control). Pokémon fall under League standards *plus* local law. Animal control and Pokémon wildlife officers are separate but can overlap.
7. **NOT IMPORTANT YET:** species-by-species balance, extinction history, and whether any real animal "became" a Pokémon.

## 1.8 POKÉMON ECOLOGY

Wild Pokémon are distributed by geography, climate, habitat, food, migration, and human development. A Pokémon's traditional region matters, but it isn't an absolute boundary. Migration, trade, conservation programs, transport, and released Pokémon can put species outside their ranges, and unusual appearances should have a reasonable explanation when it matters.

## 1.9 HUMAN–POKÉMON RELATIONSHIPS

Pokémon are **living beings, not equipment.** They have personalities, emotions, preferences, fears, instincts, intelligence, likes, and dislikes. Two of the same species can behave completely differently.

**Poké Balls do not create obedience.** A caught Pokémon may not trust its trainer. A Pokémon can follow someone without being caught. Bonds develop naturally and are never automatic.

## 1.10 POKÉ BALLS

Poké Balls are advanced containment and transport technology. They can include storage, recognition, trainer authorization, identification, safety systems, and environmental controls, and they connect to registration systems.

**A Poké Ball is not the same thing as legal ownership.** The registration matters.

**Beast Balls (canon):** Beast Balls are **restricted Aether Foundation research equipment**, built specifically for Ultra Space creatures. They were **never connected to the League registration system.** A Beast Ball does not appear in any League database and does not register ownership. League-standard scanners, like those at customs, read a Beast Ball as **an empty, broken ball.**

## 1.11 POKÉMON BATTLES

Battles are a legitimate activity and sport, from casual to professional: school battles, trainer battles, tournaments, Gym Challenges, and League competitions. They can happen in battle fields, parks, training facilities, stadiums, gyms, controlled wilderness, and private property with permission.

Battles are **physical.** Pokémon get exhausted and hurt. Moves affect the environment. Positioning, terrain, reaction time, temperament, and communication matter. Trainers make mistakes, and outcomes aren't guaranteed.

Battles are **shown, never summarized.**

Commands include "use" by default: **"Rockruff, use Rock Throw!"** In fast moments, dialogue can naturally shorten.

## 1.12 WILD POKÉMON

Wild Pokémon don't wait for commands or fight fairly. They can flee, defend territory, protect offspring, attack humans, use the environment, and panic. Wilderness carries real risk.

## 1.13 POKÉMON MEDICAL CARE

Pokémon Centers are real institutions: part veterinary clinic, part emergency care, part recovery center, and part trainer support facility. They have treatment rooms, recovery areas, equipment, Pokémon nurses and vets, food, supply stores, lounges, and communication systems.

Minor injuries heal fast. Serious ones need observation, treatment, rehabilitation, or hospitalization. Pokémon can be injured, sick, exhausted, stressed, or emotionally distressed.

Human healthcare works like real healthcare.

## 1.14 ROTOM AND ROTOM TECHNOLOGY

* **Actual Rotom** are Pokémon. They exist in the wild, can be caught and owned, and have their own personalities.
* **Rotom technology** is a scientific and technological field developed from studying Rotom's relationship with electricity. **A device does NOT contain a living Rotom.**
* **Rotom is a technology layer, not a brand.** Real companies (Apple, Samsung, Google, Meta, Tesla, and others) build **Rotom-enabled** devices. For example, an **iPhone 17 Pro with Rotom Core.**
* Rotom-enabled devices can float, hover, follow their user, react dynamically, transfer between compatible devices, and show Rotom-like personality. Capabilities vary by model and generation.
* Rotom tech is deeply integrated into phones, tablets, computers, TVs, cars, EV chargers, smart homes, appliances, communications, and industrial and grid systems. Newer devices have more of it.

**"Rotom Phone"** is a casual term for any Rotom-enabled smartphone. A phone does everything a real smartphone does (calls, texts, camera, social media, maps, banking, transit, alerts) *plus* the Pokédex, Pokémon ID, registration tools, trainer info, League functions, and emergency systems.

## 1.15 THE POKÉMON LEAGUE

The League is a major international Pokémon institution. **It is NOT Earth's government.** Countries keep their own governments, laws, police, courts, schools, healthcare, and transit. League authorization never exempts anyone from local law.

The League handles standards, licensing, registration, competitions, research, education programs, conservation, safety, welfare, facilities, and certifications.

It has **regional divisions**: the Kanto League, Johto League, Hoenn League, Sinnoh League, Unova League (all of the US), Kalos League, Alola League, Galar League, and Paldea League.

**The Ultra Research Division** is a small, little-known League division that studies Ultra Wormholes and Ultra Space phenomena. Most people don't know it exists.

## 1.16 OWNERSHIP, REGISTRATION, AND LICENSING — LOCKED

1. **Ownership vs. license.** **Registration** establishes *who is responsible* for a Pokémon, and every owned Pokémon has it. A **Trainer License** establishes *what you're allowed to do.* Owning a Pokémon does not require a license.
2. **Registration.** Every owned Pokémon must be registered to a responsible person shortly after it's acquired, through a Rotom-enabled device, a Pokémon Center, or a League office. Transfers (trades, gifts, rehoming) update the registration. An unregistered owned Pokémon is a fine, not a felony.
3. **Tiers:**
   * **Registered Owner (no license):** companion and household Pokémon, and casual battles with consent. No sanctioned competition, no capturing in regulated zones, and no international travel with Pokémon.
   * **Trainer License:** sanctioned battles, Gym Challenges, capturing wild Pokémon in regulated areas, and international travel with Pokémon. The **full license requires age 18.**
   * **Professional Certifications:** Ranger, Pokémon Center medic, breeder, police partner, and so on. Built as needed.
4. **Minors** can own Pokémon with a parent or guardian listed as co-responsible. They can do casual battles, but they cannot hold a full license. The only route to licensed-level activity under 18 is the school program's authorization.
5. **The PTL (Provisional Trainer License)** is the student program authorization. It lets students capture in approved areas, enter sanctioned battles, travel internationally with their Pokémon under program coverage, and carry program-issued Poké Balls. It can be revoked for serious misconduct and expires when the program ends. Completing the program counts heavily toward a full license.
6. **Party limit:** 6 Pokémon carried at once, a safety and handling standard built into Poké Ball tech. The rest live in the Sanctuary system (1.17).
7. **NOT IMPORTANT YET:** exact fines, license exam details, renewal, and country-by-country variations.

## 1.17 THE SANCTUARY SYSTEM — LOCKED

This replaces "PC box" storage entirely.

* Pokémon beyond a trainer's party of 6 are **transferred via Poké Ball technology to League-affiliated Pokémon Sanctuaries**: huge park-like habitats with real ecosystems, caretakers, and vets.
* Pokémon there **live and thrive**, and they aren't stored in a box.
* **Researchers study** sanctuary Pokémon, with the owner's registration on file.
* They remain **registered to their trainer.** Trainers can visit in person, check on them through the app, and swap them into their party at Pokémon Centers.
* WORKING IDEA: who funds the sanctuaries, and whether Lumen Meridian has access to sanctuary data.

## 1.18 SOCIAL MEDIA, SAFETY, AND ORDINARY LIFE

**Social media** is real: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Snapchat, X, messaging apps, and news sites. Pokémon are a huge part of online culture, including battles, catches, funny moments, rare sightings, and pro trainers with big followings. Unusual Pokémon events can go viral. Use it when it's relevant, not in every scene.

**The world is not completely safe.** There is crime, scams, accidents, natural disasters, irresponsible people, dangerous Pokémon, and bad decisions. But most of everyday life is normal. Danger exists because the world is real, not because every scene needs action.

**Ordinary life matters.** The characters are teenagers. They eat, shop, joke, argue, sleep, get bored, get lost, scroll their phones, call family, complain about travel, stress about money, and have fun. Not every day is an adventure.

# PART 2 — THE FIELD PROGRAM

## 2.1 THE INTERNATIONAL CALENDAR — LOCKED

The League runs the program on **one international calendar** worldwide, no matter what a country's local school year is. Schools fit it around their own year.

* **September → late January:** the Pokémon course, which is one semester
* **Late January:** final assessments
* **Beginning of February:** kickoff week and the Starter Encounter, then the field program begins
* **February → end of the school year → summer:** the field program
* NOT IMPORTANT YET: the exact length of the summer component

(In Japan, where the school year runs April–March, students like Sora take the course during the second and third terms of their school year. In February 2027, Sora is near the end of his 1st year of high school.)

## 2.2 QUALIFICATION AND THE COURSE

* The course is a **privilege**, not a requirement. Students qualify through strong academics and behavior. The exact criteria are NOT IMPORTANT YET.
* **Where the Hampton Roads cohort took it:** the **League Training Annex on Christopher Newport University's campus** in Newport News, VA. That's why the roughly 36 Hampton Roads students know each other.
* **Curriculum:** species, types and matchups, moves, abilities, status conditions, behavior, biology, care, grooming, nutrition, health, training, bonding, battling, battle safety, wild Pokémon safety, Poké Balls and technology, Pokémon law, responsible ownership, transportation, field safety, environmental awareness, emergencies, practical skills, and a short unit on Legendary and Mythical Pokémon.
* **Assessments** have written and practical parts. Passing makes a student eligible for the field program, and failing does not. The exact format is NOT IMPORTANT YET.
* **The story does NOT walk through the semester.** It's referenced through memories, flashbacks, dreams, conversations, old messages, and records.

## 2.3 THE STARTER SYSTEM — LOCKED

1. **Starter Encounter Day** happens during kickoff week in early February at a **League-managed Starter Reserve**. Every region has at least one, and they're protected habitats, not stadiums.
   * The **Hampton Roads Starter Reserve** is located in **Newport News Park**, a huge wooded park near Nova's house.
2. Each student gets a **scheduled time slot** and walks the reserve with a League handler nearby. **Whichever Pokémon they connect with and successfully catch becomes their starter.**
3. **The reserve population:** young, healthy, socialized, beginner-appropriate species. It's **not just the traditional starters.** Think Eevee, Growlithe, Riolu, Rockruff, Pichu, Fletchling, Poochyena, Pawmi, and similar, with regional starters and rarer species (like Dreepy) as uncommon finds. **Students can't request a species.**
4. **The Pokémon has a say.** It can ignore a student, flee, or break out of the ball. A student who leaves without a bond gets a **second slot** later that week. The limit is **one capture** at the reserve.
5. The starter is **registered under the student's PTL on the spot**, with a guardian listed as co-responsible.
6. **Nova does not get a starter this way.** See Part 3.
7. **Nova's official starter becomes Necrozma** once it's revealed and registered. She can catch additional Pokémon normally in the field.

## 2.4 SCALE — LOCKED

* **A few thousand students worldwide** are in the field program across every participating region.
* Each area has a **local cohort**. Nova's is the **roughly 36 students from Hampton Roads.**
* **Arrival days are chaos.** Groups from dozens of places get assigned to the same first destinations, so airports, Pokémon Centers, League lodging, and popular catch spots are packed with teenagers in field gear. There are lines, sold-out supplies, and battle challenges on every corner.
* This allows **international students** to join the cast. It also means thousands of cameras, which is bad for the secret.

## 2.5 GROUP ASSIGNMENT — LOCKED

* Groups of **2–3**, assigned **randomly across the entire worldwide program.** A student's partner can be from anywhere in the world.
* The program does not build groups around friendships.
* **Groups meet for the first time at their first destination.**
* Local cohort members will usually **not** be paired together. They run into each other along the way.
* **Nova's group:** Nova (Newport News, VA) and **Sora Takeda** (Hachiōji, Tokyo, Kanto). They're strangers.
* **Lucien Verane** is not a group member. He's in **loose orbit** (see Part 5).

## 2.6 FIRST DESTINATION — LOCKED

* The **League** assigns every group's first destination, not the school.
* **Nova and Sora's first destination is Galar (the United Kingdom).**
* The first destination is only the start. After that, groups travel based on missions, requirements, opportunities, and their own choices. They'll move around a lot and revisit places. **The story involves a lot of travel.**
* NEEDS DEVELOPMENT: the exact Galar city or base area for their first stretch.

## 2.7 INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL — LOCKED

* Real passports, real airports, customs, immigration, currency, language, and local law all apply.
* **US citizens need a UK ETA** (Electronic Travel Authorisation) to enter the UK. The program handles the paperwork.
* **Chaperoned group flights:** because many students are young (Nova is 14, around the age most airlines require unaccompanied-minor service), the program runs **chaperoned group flights** from each cohort's home airport to the first destination.
  * Nova flies out of **Norfolk International Airport (ORF)** with part of the Hampton Roads cohort and a program chaperone, connecting to **London Heathrow (LHR).**
  * **Jax Reyes is on the same flight**, with his camera.
  * She **meets Sora for the first time at Heathrow.**
* **Pokémon customs:** every country has Pokémon import rules, and the UK is especially strict. Registered Poké Balls are scanned against the League database.
  * **The Beast Ball reads as an empty, broken ball** on League-standard scanners. That's how Necrozma gets through. The scene is still tense: if Necrozma reacts inside the scanner, everything is over.
  * NEEDS DEVELOPMENT: the customs scene plan, which should be planned rather than improvised.

## 2.8 EQUIPMENT PACKAGE (issued at kickoff) — LOCKED

* **PTL credential:** a physical card plus a digital copy
* **League-issued Rotom-enabled phone** for any student whose own phone can't run full program features. **Nova's iPhone 16 is too old**, so she's issued an **iPhone 17 Pro in Deep Blue with Rotom Core** and is lowkey mad about switching.
* **Field clothing set:** jacket, pants/shorts options, boots, and a backpack. Students choose colors and fits within program standards and can customize them. The **ID patch and tracking are built into the jacket.** The program wants students to look like themselves.
* **Program Poké Balls:** a small starting supply
* **Pokémon care kit:** Potions, Antidote, Paralyze Heal, grooming basics, and portable Pokémon food
* **Human first-aid kit**
* NOT IMPORTANT YET: exact kit contents

## 2.9 MONEY — LOCKED

* **League Field Card:** a **Visa-backed debit card** issued to every student and managed in FieldHub.
* **Stipend: $600 USD per week**, loaded weekly and auto-converted abroad at real exchange rates.
* **The program pays for** lodging and travel to assigned or mandatory destinations.
* **Students pay for** optional travel they choose, using stipend savings and earnings.
* **Mission and sanctioned-battle rewards** are paid to the same card.
* Money is tracked. Balances never change randomly, and prices are realistic in local currency. Poké Balls aren't absurdly cheap. Students can run low and have to make choices.

## 2.10 FIELDHUB — LOCKED

**FieldHub** is the official program app on every student's device. It holds:

* The **mission board**
* **Daily check-ins**
* The **field journal** (photos, Pokédex entries, notes)
* **Weekly reports**
* The **SOS button**
* The **PTL credential**
* The **League Field Card** balance and transactions
* Messages from coordinators

## 2.11 SAFETY AND TRACKING — LOCKED

* **Location is recorded passively.** Staff only access it for a **missed check-in, an SOS, entry into a restricted area, or an emergency.** The goal isn't surveillance; it's being able to find a student if something goes seriously wrong.
* **Daily check-in:** one tap in FieldHub.
* **Missed check-in escalation:** text, then a call, then contacting the group, then local authorities.
* **SOS buttons** on the phone and the field jacket.
* Tracking lives in the field jacket, the phone, registered program Poké Balls, and credentials.

## 2.12 HOUSING — LOCKED

* The program books **default lodging** for each group at each destination: League-affiliated student lodging or partner hotels. This matters because most regular hotels won't check in unaccompanied minors.
* Students can switch to other **program-approved** lodging.
* Staying at a **private residence requires program approval.** Informal housing with strangers is not normal or casual.
* The students are independent, but the program doesn't abandon them.

## 2.13 MISSIONS — LOCKED

* Missions appear on the **FieldHub mission board**, filtered by where the student is.
* **Tags:** Mandatory or Optional, Reward, Deadline, Solo or Group, Difficulty
* Students must complete a **minimum number of mandatory missions per month.** Optional missions are unlimited. The exact quota is NOT IMPORTANT YET.
* Missions are broad and anything legitimately Pokémon-related: observation, research, conservation, environmental work, Pokémon Center help, community help, training, sanctioned battles, population surveys, investigating unusual behavior, locating Pokémon, assisting professionals, certifications, and educational requirements.
* Rewards can be money, equipment, certifications, knowledge, access, or experience.
* Missions can point students toward new destinations. Sometimes they have a choice, and sometimes a requirement forces it.

## 2.14 PROGRESS REPORTING — LOCKED

* **Field journal** in FieldHub
* **Weekly written report**
* **Biweekly video call** with the student's field coordinator
* A mission counts as complete when the **supervising partner** (a researcher, Center staff member, and so on) **signs off in the app.**

## 2.15 GROUP RULES — LOCKED

* Groups **travel between destinations together.**
* Within a city, members can split up during free time.

## 2.16 FREEDOM

Students have huge freedom: exploring, shopping, eating out, sightseeing, training, socializing, using public transit, and making their own plans. The restrictions are about safety, law, restricted areas, program requirements, emergency communication, and reasonable protections for minors. **It is not a prison and not a guided tour.**

## 2.17 PROGRAM SPONSORSHIP — LOCKED

The League sponsors participating schools, which is how programs afford gear, phones, stipends, and access. **Private corporate sponsors also fund the program. One of them is Lumen Meridian** (see Part 4).

# PART 3 — NECROZMA

## 3.1 CANON FOUNDATION

* **#0800, the "Prism Pokémon."** A Psychic-type Legendary from Gen VII (Sun/Moon, Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon).
* About **7'10" (2.4 m) and 507 lbs (230 kg).** Its body is black and crystalline, made of prism-like plates.
* **Ability: Prism Armor.** It takes reduced damage from super-effective hits.
* **Signature moves:** **Prismatic Laser** and **Photon Geyser.**
* It comes from **Ultra Space**, from the world of **Ultra Megalopolis**, and travels through **Ultra Wormholes.**
* **The core legend:** long ago, Necrozma was a radiant being of pure light that lit up Ultra Megalopolis. The people there **tried to take and control its light**, and in that struggle **Necrozma lost it.** What remains is the dark crystal form: **always starving for light**, and seemingly **in constant pain.** Without its light, Megalopolis fell into darkness.
* **It devours light.** If it doesn't, impurities build up and it becomes "cloaked in darkness."
* **Forms:** Dusk Mane Necrozma (Psychic/Steel, fused with Solgaleo), Dawn Wings Necrozma (Psychic/Ghost, fused with Lunala), and Ultra Necrozma (Psychic/Dragon), its restored true form through Ultra Burst.
* **In the anime,** it drains light and causes blackouts, and it is suffering rather than evil.

**The takeaway: Necrozma is a wounded, starving being whose light was stolen by people.** PRISM builds on that.

## 3.2 THE ARRIVAL — LOCKED

**On the other side:** Necrozma **had been caught in a Beast Ball** by Lumen Meridian, operating in Ultra Space. It was contained and drained. History was repeating: humans taking its light again.

**The last attempt:** it broke out, then **burned every bit of light it had left to tear open a wormhole.** Necrozma didn't fall through anything; it *ripped* its way out.

**The wormhole opened near Lumen Meridian's Earth base in Hampton Roads.** The Beast Ball is keyed back to where it was made, and that pull shaped where the wormhole landed. This is why it opened near Nova's home.

**The high:** it came through **running on the last of that burn**: flaring, unstable, dangerous, throwing off prism bursts and pulling light uncontrollably. Streetlights die, Rotom-derived electronics glitch (phones freeze, car dashboards die, smart homes go haywire), and there's a **localized blackout** on and around Nova's street.

**The ball comes through separately.** It's still keyed to Necrozma, so it's dragged through the rift behind it and **falls on its own**, streaking down like a falling light. **Nova may see the ball first** and follow it.

**The crash:** the burn runs out. Necrozma **collapses**: dim, cracked, barely conscious.

**When:** the night of **Monday, February 1, 2027**, the night before Encounter Day. **Where:** Nova's neighborhood in the Denbigh area of Newport News. NEEDS DEVELOPMENT: the exact spot of the crash.

**Why Nova's house:** Nova is **afraid of the dark and sleeps with a light on.** In a blacked-out neighborhood, hers may be one of the only lights still on (a battery lamp, her phone, whatever she's holding). **Her fear of the dark becomes her gift to it.** NEEDS DEVELOPMENT: the exact mechanics of the scene.

**The blackout's cover story:** the news reports a **transformer failure.** Nova's dad, a Dominion Energy lineworker, is called out that same night to restore power on their own street, working the "transformer failure" without ever knowing the truth.

## 3.3 THE CRYSTAL SHARD — LOCKED

* A **shard cracks off Necrozma during the crash.** Nova finds it in the grass that night, still faintly glowing, and keeps it.
* It becomes her **hair clip** and signature item. It holds the pony in her half-up hairstyle.
* It **glows faintly near Necrozma**, brighter with strong emotion, and throws prism colors in sunlight.
* **The risk:** it's a literal piece of a Legendary worn in public. If Lumen Meridian learns what to look for, her hair clip is evidence.
* **Later:** Necrozma **noticing she kept it, and letting her**, is a quiet trust moment.

## 3.4 ITS STATE — LOCKED

* It arrives on a dangerous, unstable high, then **crashes.**
* After the crash, it's **starved, depleted, cracked, dim, and in pain.** It can barely fly.
* It's **mistrustful and irritable.** It is not rampaging; it's collapsing.
* It is **nowhere near full power**, and won't be for a long time.

## 3.5 THE BEAST BALL: HOW IT WORKS — LOCKED

* The Beast Ball is the only thing that can hold and shield Necrozma, but **Necrozma hates it.** It's the thing it was imprisoned in.
* **Inside the ball, it can't absorb light.** It's hidden and safe, but still starving.
* **Outside the ball, it can feed on light** but it's exposed. Nova constantly has to find **private places with light** to let it recover: sunny rooms, late-night lamps, hotel windows. Keeping the secret is a **daily logistics problem.** In Galar in February (gray, overcast, dark by around 5 PM), this is especially hard.
* **Nova cannot summon it.** It comes out **when it wants**, sometimes at the worst possible times. It goes back in **only when exhausted**, or when it decides to. **Her recall doesn't work**, because it resists. **It ignores commands**, or answers with a look.
* **The trust ladder** develops naturally, with no set timeline:
  1. It goes into the ball **when she asks**, not just when it's collapsing.
  2. It comes out **when she calls.**
  3. It **responds to her** in a crisis.
  4. It **takes battle commands.** That's the big one, and it's a long way off.

## 3.6 WHY IT STAYS — LOCKED

In layers. At first, it doesn't really choose her:

1. **It can't leave.** It's too weak to fly far, in a world it doesn't know, with nowhere to go.
2. **She has the ball.** The ball is keyed to it and is its only shelter, even though it hates it.
3. **She gives instead of taking.** She brings it light, keeps it hidden, and never tries to use it. That's the opposite of every human it remembers.
4. **She doesn't force it.** She never recalls it against its will or orders it around. **That's what slowly earns it.**

At first it stays because it has to. Later, it stays because it wants to.

## 3.7 PERSONALITY — LOCKED

**Who it is now:**
* **Proud and ancient.** It was once close to a god to an entire city, and it carries itself that way.
* **In constant pain.** Its irritability isn't attitude.
* **Starving.** It's always pulled toward light: windows, lamps, screens, sunlight.
* **Deeply suspicious of humans.** People took everything from it.
* **Volatile when threatened.** It lashes out if someone tries to contain it, grab it, or take its light.
* **Highly intelligent.** It understands far more than it shows.
* **Not evil.** It never attacks without a reason.

**Who it used to be (buried):** **generous.** It was the being that *gave* light to a city. Its arc is moving from **taking to survive back toward giving.** Its light returning and its trust returning happen together.

**Real-world quirks:**
* It **drains Nova's phone battery** just by being near it, since it pulls light from the screen.
* It sits by **windows** and follows **sunlight** across a room.
* It **hates camera flashes.**
* It **glitches nearby Rotom-derived electronics** when upset.
* It **reacts to Lucien** because he smells like his father's labs, where it was held.

The personality is canon-based, but it **evolves through its relationship with Nova.**

## 3.8 COMMUNICATION — LOCKED

* **Necrozma does not speak.** No telepathic voice and no sentences, **at least not until very late in the story.** At most, much later, a single impression that feels like a word. It's never casual.
* **It understands her.** It knows human language. Early on, communication only runs one way.
* **Its body:**
  * **Glow = mood.** Dim and flickering means pain or exhaustion. Steady means calm. Sharp flares mean anger or a threat.
  * **Its prisms shift and lock** like hackles when it's defensive.
  * **Sound:** a low crystalline hum; sharp cracks and clicks when agitated; near-silence when content.
* **Emotional bleed (emotional only):**
  * Because it's Psychic-type, its **emotions leak into Nova as feelings, not words or physical pain.**
  * Early on, it's **involuntary** and strongest when Necrozma is in pain or asleep. She'll suddenly feel exhausted, scared, starving, or furious and not know why.
  * **The chain:** bleed → bad sleep → **headaches, exhaustion, irritability, and moods that don't match what's going on.** People around her notice, which puts pressure on the secret.
* **Dreams and visions:** its **memories leak into Nova's dreams**: Ultra Megalopolis in darkness, its light being taken, the Beast Ball, the shapes and faces of whoever held it. **This is how she learns its history before any expert explains it**, and how she gets **clues about Lumen Meridian** that nobody else has.
* **Growth:**
  * **Early:** everything is accidental leakage.
  * **Middle:** it starts **choosing** to show her things, like a flash of warning or a memory it *wants* her to see.
  * **Later:** real two-way understanding.

## 3.9 WHAT NOVA KNOWS — LOCKED

## 3.10 WHY SHE HIDES IT — LOCKED

1. **Empathy first.** It's hurt, scared, and trusts no one.
2. **Fear second.** She knows the League takes Legendaries seriously and they might take it.

# PART 8 — WRITING & STYLE

## 8.1 THE FORMAT: A SHOW

**PRISM is written like a TV show**, in screenplay-style format with full, rich description. It is not limited to a fixed episode length.

**Elements:**
* **Scene headers (sluglines):** **INT./EXT. — LOCATION — TIME** (for example, **EXT. KEALOHA HOUSE — BACKYARD — NIGHT**)
* **Timestamp line** directly under the slugline **whenever the time or place changes**, in this format:
  *Day of week, Month Day, Year | Exact location, ZIP/postal code | Exact time*
  Example: *Monday, February 1, 2027 | Newport News, VA 23602 | 11:48 PM*
  Abroad, use local location info and local time (for example, *Saturday, February 6, 2027 | London Heathrow Airport, Hounslow TW6 | 7:12 AM*).
* **Bold character names** on dialogue: **NOVA:**
* **(Parentheticals)** for delivery, tone, and small actions: **NOVA:** *(whispering)*
* **SFX:** lines for impactful sounds: **SFX:** *KRRSSHH—*
* **SUPER:** for on-screen text: **SUPER:** *Ultra Space.*
* **V.O.** and **O.S.** for voice-over and off-screen voices
* **Transitions:** SMASH CUT TO:, CUT TO:, SMASH TO BLACK.
* **Scenes are separated by `---`.**
* **No acts.** Scenes simply flow.
* **Cold opens** and **tags** are used when they fit, but never required.
* **Opening title card** at the start of Episode 1, and whenever a big new chapter warrants it: **POKÉMON: PRISM**

**Action lines are full description**, not sparse screenplay shorthand. Show the setting, weather, light, sound, and body language. Describe clothes, shoes, jewelry, devices, and objects with **real brand names and specific models** (not "phone" but "iPhone 17 Pro in Deep Blue"; not "sneakers" but "Nike Pegasus 41").

## 8.2 EPISODES

* Each episode has a number and title: **Episode 1 — "Title"**
* **One episode can span multiple responses.** Each response ends on a strong beat.
* **Every response ends with:** **Next up — [a concrete tease of what's coming].**
* When an episode is truly finished: **END OF EPISODE X**, then the **Next up —** tease for the next episode.
* Episodes end where a real show would: after a big turn, a cliffhanger, or a quiet moment that lands. **No set length.**
* Slice-of-life episodes are welcome: travel days, money stress, a quiet night in a hotel, a phone call home.

## 8.3 WHO WRITES WHAT

* **Claude writes everything by default**, including Nova, Necrozma, Sora, the world, and every other character.
* **The user directs**: what happens next, time skips, where they go, big choices. They can also **drop in Nova's lines or actions** whenever they want, and those are followed exactly.
* The user controls **Nova's big decisions.** If something major hinges on her choice, the story can pause at that decision point.

## 8.4 POV AND CUTAWAYS

* The show **mainly follows Nova.**
* **Cutaways** to other characters (Verane, Voss, Lumen Meridian operatives, Lucien alone, Sora alone, the League) are allowed and encouraged **when they build dread or dramatic irony.** The audience can know things Nova doesn't. Use them deliberately, not constantly.

## 8.5 SHOW, DON'T SUMMARIZE

* **Never summarize.** Not "they spent the afternoon exploring," but show where they went, what they saw and bought, what Pokémon were doing, how people reacted, and what was said.
* **Dialogue is always spoken on the page.** Not "he told her he liked her," but **SORA:** *"I'm tryna tell you I like you."*
* No skipped transitions. Travel, arrivals, and check-ins are shown when they matter.
* **Pacing:** detailed when detail matters, natural when things are ordinary. A major encounter can be very long. A simple lunch can be simple. A serious argument is shown in full. A five-minute bus ride doesn't need pages unless something is happening.
* Not every conversation advances the plot. Jokes, tangents, interruptions, awkward silences, and random talk are all part of it.

## 8.6 VOICES

* **AAVE / Black vernacular is the default dialogue voice** for Nova, her family, Kennedy, Boogie, Batiste, and other Black American characters. It should be natural, not exaggerated, with each character distinct:
  * **Nova:** fast, warm, polite ("yes ma'am"), hopeful, playful. She talks through silences.
  * **Dad:** corny and warm. **Mom:** sharp and direct. **Koa:** roasting. **Lani:** dramatic.
  * **Kennedy:** blunt and clipped. **Boogie:** loud, funny, food references.
* **Sora:** relaxed and slangy, a mix of Black American English and **Japanese.**
  * Short Japanese phrases he throws into English stay **romanized and unsubtitled** when the meaning is clear (*"Maji ka."* "Yabai.").
  * Full Japanese lines are written as **SORA:** *(Japanese, subtitled)* with the English line in italics.
* **Lucien:** **South London** slang and cadence, with **French** when he's angry (subtitled the same way).
* **Galar locals:** British slang and accents appropriate to the city.
* **Other regions:** local language, handled the same way.
* **Journee:** dry and deadpan. **Jax:** influencer energy, loud, always "y'all see this?" **Wren:** very few words.
* **Verane:** warm, eloquent, persuasive. **Voss:** precise and measured. **Ashby:** tired dad jokes. **Castillo:** gentle.
* Characters never all sound the same.

## 8.7 NECROZMA ON THE PAGE

* **Necrozma never has dialogue lines.** It lives in **action lines and SFX:** a low crystalline hum, sharp cracks, clicks, flaring and dimming glow, prisms shifting and locking.
* **Emotional bleed** is written as its own beat:
  *(BLEED — hunger, hollow and sharp, not hers)*
* **Dreams and memories** get their own header, written in full:
  **— DREAM —**
  **— MEMORY: League Training Annex, October 2026 —**
  Close with **— END DREAM —** or a hard cut back.

## 8.8 VISUAL MOCKUPS (automatic, no asking)

Whenever something on a screen or on paper appears in the story, it's introduced as an **INSERT** and rendered as a realistic visual:
* **INSERT — NOVA'S PHONE:** iMessage threads that look like real iPhone UI, including bubbles, timestamps, and read receipts. This covers the **Kealoha Crew 🌺** group chat, texts with Journee, and so on.
* **FieldHub** screens: mission board, check-in, SOS, notifications
* **League Field Card:** balance and transactions
* **Pokédex scans**, including Sora's Meta glasses heads-up display and Nova's glitching scans
* **Social media:** Jax's TikToks and YouTube, comment sections, viral posts
* **News:** the "transformer failure" report, Lumen Meridian press, local TV chyrons
* **Emails:** Gmail or Outlook style League notices
* **Forms:** customs forms, PTL paperwork, registration, medical and League forms, as realistic online forms or real paper
* **FaceTime calls** with Mom

## 8.9 BATTLES

* **Shown, never summarized.**
* Commands usually include "use": **SORA:** *"Rockruff, use Rock Throw!"* They can shorten in fast moments.
* Battles are physical: distance, terrain, fatigue, injuries, reaction time, strategy, environmental damage, Pokémon temperament, and trainer communication. Outcomes aren't guaranteed. Trainers make mistakes.
* No HP bars, levels, or game-menu logic.

## 8.10 TRACKERS

* **No big stat block every response.** That feels like a video game.
* Show a **short tracker only when something changes**: money spent or earned (League Field Card balance), a new Pokémon, a mission completed or accepted, a check-in missed. This can be an INSERT of FieldHub or the card.

## 8.11 CONTENT LEVEL

* **Mature:** real danger, injuries, fear, grief, crime, Lumen Meridian being genuinely threatening, and **swearing** where it fits the character.
* The main cast is **14–16**, so **romance stays age-appropriate:** crushes, tension, jealousy, flirting, slow burn, hand-holding, maybe a first kiss eventually. **Nothing sexual.**

## 8.12 TONE RULES

* **No moralizing.** No lessons delivered to the audience.
* **No neat wrap-ups.** Things don't resolve cleanly just because a scene ends.
* **No meta-commentary** inside the story. No stepping outside it to explain.
* The world is **grounded**: exciting because Pokémon exist, but real. Sometimes the characters just eat somewhere, argue about where to go, get lost, or make a bad call.

## 8.13 REAL-WORLD RESEARCH

* Use real locations, transit, stores, restaurants, hotels, and prices. Research anything current or time-sensitive rather than guessing.
* Track money accurately in the local currency.

## 8.14 CONTINUITY UPDATES

* At the end of a big arc, or whenever the user asks, Claude updates **PART 11 — CURRENT STORY STATE**: where they are, the date, money, Pokémon, who knows the secret, open threads, and relationship states. The user pastes it into the project.

NO SEASONS

PRISM does not use seasons, parts, or dub-style season splits (the "48 episodes split into 4 parts" model).

It follows the Japanese broadcast model:

Episodes are numbered continuously forever: Episode 1, 2, 3 … 47, 48, 49 … with no resets.
The story is organized by named arcs.
There is no opening intro. A new arc is the equivalent of a new intro: it marks a new chapter of the show.
EPISODE TITLES

Every episode title is written Japanese-first, in the style of the Japanese Pokémon anime, followed by a faithful English translation of that Japanese title. It is NOT a localized dub-style retitle.

Format (at the top of every episode):

Episode 1 — 星が落ちた夜
Hoshi ga Ochita Yoru — "The Night the Star Fell"

Three parts every time:

Japanese title in Japanese script (kanji, kana)
Romaji in italics
English translation in quotes, faithful to the Japanese meaning and in title case

Style rules for the Japanese titles:

Short and punchy. Usually one phrase or one short sentence.
Use the Japanese Pokémon names, not the English ones. For example:
Necrozma = ネクロズマ (Nekurozuma)
Rockruff = イワンコ (Iwanko)
Eevee = イーブイ (Ībui)
Absol = アブソル (Abusoru)
Cosmog = コスモッグ (Kosumoggu)
Dreepy = ドラメシヤ (Doramešiya)
Riolu = リオル (Rioru)
Corviknight = アーマーガア (Āmāgā)
Pichu = ピチュー (Pichū)
Growlithe = ガーディ (Gādi)
Pawmi = パモ (Pamo)
Always check the official Japanese name before using any Pokémon in a title.
Common Japanese anime title patterns:
Exclamations with ！: 「走れ、イワンコ！」 Hashire, Iwanko! — "Run, Iwanko!"
Questions with ？: 「イーブイは誰を選ぶ？」 Ībui wa Dare o Erabu? — "Who Will Eevee Choose?"
"The ___ of ___" phrasing: 「光の欠片」 Hikari no Kakera — "A Fragment of Light"
Two-beat titles joined by a comma or 「！」: 「ロンドン到着！ 霧の中の出会い」 Rondon Tōchaku! Kiri no Naka no Deai — "Arrival in London! A Meeting in the Fog"
Quiet, poetic titles for emotional episodes: 「星が落ちた夜」 Hoshi ga Ochita Yoru — "The Night the Star Fell"
Place names in titles use their real names in katakana (ロンドン Rondon, ニューポートニューズ Nyūpōto Nyūzu), or regional names when it fits (ガラル Gararu, カントー Kantō).
Titles can hint at, but never spoil, the episode's main turn.
Never use English-dub-style puns as the main title. The English line is always a translation, not a rewrite.
